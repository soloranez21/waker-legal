1. What the App does — and what it doesn't promise

Waker is an alarm clock that keeps ringing until your iPhone's camera has verified a morning mission. There are five missions: finding a household object the App picks at random, push-ups, squats, a plank hold, and brushing your teeth. A routine can contain up to three missions in a row. No photo is saved.

Your iPhone's system stop control (or a hardware button) always ends the current ring; the App cannot prevent that. If the mission isn't done, Waker rings again. An unfinished mission stops ringing on its own after about 30 minutes.

It is a convenience and motivation tool, not a safety-critical system: do not rely on it as your only safeguard where oversleeping could cause harm or loss. Alarms only work if your device is on and charged and you have allowed alarms for the App. Your iPhone's Ringtone and Alerts volume and your iOS settings control how loud the alarm is. You are responsible for checking these settings and permissions; the App checks them where it can but cannot guarantee them.

2. Health and safety

The exercise missions are exercise. The App does not give medical advice. Consult a professional before starting an exercise routine if you have any condition that makes exercise risky. Stop any exercise mission right away if you feel unwell, dizzy, or in pain. Take care when you get up and move around, especially in the dark. Choose a supported repetition count or hold duration, or choose a mission that requires no exercise, such as finding an object. Brushing verification checks visible movement, not how well you clean your teeth.
